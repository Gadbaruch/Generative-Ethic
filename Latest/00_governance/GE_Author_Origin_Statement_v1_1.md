# Who I Am, and Why I Am Doing This
### A Personal Statement — Gad Bruch Hinkis

---

## Admissions First

I want to begin with the things I cannot claim.

I have not read almost any books in my life. I barely graduated high school — in the art department. I have no university degree, no academic credentials, no institutional position. I am not a philosopher by training, and I have not earned that title.

What I am, professionally, is a music producer and music-technology entrepreneur. I co-founded two startups — Jambl and PatchXR — that I would describe as not greatly successful. I have spent my adult life in creative technology: building tools for making music, thinking about participation and sound.

My main way of receiving information is not text. It is voice — conversations, debates, podcasts, YouTube, the right people talking about the right things. Reading and writing were always a struggle for me. The thinkers who shaped my understanding of the world mostly arrived through my ears. This is not a boast. It is just how my mind works.

My father and online conversations with smart people are my education and input mechanism. AI is my redemption as an output mechanism. I know it is not cool to write with AI at this stage of the adoption curve — but without it I would not have the ability to take that initial spark and put it into a format that can be absorbed by others. The ideas are mine. The framework is mine. The arguments, challenges, revisions, decisions about what to keep and discard — these are mine. But the final form in which they appear on the page would not exist without this tool. The alternative to AI-assisted writing is not better writing. It is silence.

And yet I have spent thirty years carrying an idea I cannot put down — an idea that arrived in a single night when I was twelve years old, and which has not stopped unfolding since.

This document is an attempt to say who I am, where the idea came from, and why — despite every reasonable objection — I am choosing to put it forward.

---

## My Father

My father is the academic I am not.

He studied mathematics, Jewish philosophy, poetry, and literature. He is an avid reader — books were his life. He has published around nine poetry books that are, to this day, beyond my ability to fully comprehend or analyse. His multidimensional grasp of language was always awe-inspiring, even if his day-to-day existence did not always produce awe. I always knew there was a vast universe in his mind that I could not fully access.

He also wrote two serious scholarly books. One is a history and analysis of the Cantor-Bernstein proof — a specific problem in the history of mathematics — examined from multiple perspectives. I don't know it exactly; I never read it. The other is a compilation and analysis of short stories by his favourite Hebrew author, David Shachar. I haven't read that either. I say this plainly, because what my father gave me was not delivered through his books. It was delivered on car rides.

In the mid-1990s, when I was around twelve, my father and I would drive every Friday from our suburb of Maccabim to Tel Aviv to see a film. He was an entrepreneur, often on business calls for part of the drive. But when he wasn't, we talked about deep ideas of all kinds.

He tried to explain set theory to me: the idea that there are different sizes of infinity, that some infinite sets are provably larger than others, that you can have a set that contains itself as a member. Little mind exercises and riddles that excited my brain. His knowledge of mathematics and literature seemed to collide in those rides and created an explosion of neuron generation that I am, in some sense, still riding.

And on one of those drives, he told me about *Tzimtzum*.

---

## The Night

Tzimtzum is a concept from Lurianic Kabbalah. The word means, roughly, *contraction* or *reduction*. The idea is this: before creation, there was only God — infinite, filling all possible space. For anything other than God to exist, God had to make room. He contracted inward, reduced Himself, created a void within Himself — and that void became the starting point of creation.

That night, lying in bed, my mind was in that space — playing with the image of that initial move. In a half-sleeping state, with great effort and focus, something happened that I still find difficult to describe precisely. What I remember is that I managed, step by step, to construct in my mind the creation of infinite reality out of that initial act. I had what felt like a logical explanation for why reality exists. I seem to remember parts of it, but the actual steps eluded me even the next morning.

I knew I had to remember something. So I got out of bed. I didn't write the full argument — I was twelve, it was late, and the steps were already leaving me. Instead I took two sheets of A4 paper and a thick marker and wrote one word on each, large enough to fill the page. The two words were:

**CONFLICT**

**FRACTAL**

I went back to sleep. I woke the next morning with the steps gone but the conclusion still present — something I could feel more than articulate. As best I can reconstruct it: *everything that can possibly exist, exists — and nothing exists — simultaneously. Reality flickers between existence and non-existence.* In those last moments before sleep, I remember seeing reality in that mode — literally flickering, presence and absence as the same underlying motion.

I don't know exactly what happened that night. I was twelve. It may have been a dream that felt like a proof. It may have been a mind that didn't have formal mathematical language finding its own path to something real. It may have been nothing at all.

What I know is that I have been following those two words ever since. *Conflict* became *connection and separation as the two fundamental forces of reality*. *Fractal* became *emergence across levels — the same structural dynamic operating at every scale*. The Generative Ethic is, among other things, my attempt to write out the argument I couldn't write that night.

## The Ego Admission

Part of me would like to believe that what happened that night was a revelation — that the experience was genuinely metaphysical, that this project has a messianic quality, that I am being called toward something larger than my own ambition. I acknowledge this openly, because I think honesty about one's drives is more useful than performing humility while those drives operate underneath.

My ego is active in this project. I want to be read. I want to be taken seriously. I want to have success and I want to be something special and influential. These are real motivations and I will not pretend otherwise.

But these motivations and my actual purpose point the same direction, and they feed each other. I want the framework to spread because I believe it contains something genuinely useful — not only because I want to be the person who made it. Both things are true, and the second does not cancel the first.

If this is a stupid dream that brings no value to anyone — if the framework is ultimately banal, or wrong, or just the self-mythology of someone who mistook excitement for insight — I will accept that without fear. I don't need the project to succeed to have been worth attempting. But trying to spread these ideas has been calling me for thirty years. Now I feel I actually have the tools to do so in a reasonable amount of time. Not doing it is no longer a stable option.

I also do this in spite of fear, not without it. I know I am crazy. I almost have no right to demand any audience — I have no real-world credentials and cannot call myself a philosopher in good standing. And yet I keep finding, when listening to smarter people than me, that parts of their ideas map precisely onto my framework. That keeps me going.

---

## What I Want

I want to be part of what happens next to humanity, and I want to help it reach its next stage of development — through music, tools, ideas, or combinations I cannot yet predict. I cannot know from the inside which of these will be most useful or most impactful, but the intention behind all of them is the same.

The framework's highest aspiration — and I say this with full recognition of how it sounds — is to serve as a bridge. Between materialism and spirituality. Between people who think the universe is made of atoms and people who think it is made of meaning. I believe these are two accounts of the same thing, and that a structural bridge between them is buildable. That a theist and an atheist can both use this framework without either having to give up their prior commitments. If that is true, and if the map is genuinely useful, then the practical consequences are real.

I fantasize that this framework can unlock something — a coalition between materialism and spirituality, a clear guide for navigating toward continual goodness improvement, and at the highest level, something that gives humanity the alignment toward godlikeness that has been missing from the scientific revolution. I see it clearly in the simulation in my head. I know some of the risks. I try to be brave.

And if I am just crazy — then at minimum, you will be entertained by the attempt.

*Gad Bruch Hinkis, 2026*
