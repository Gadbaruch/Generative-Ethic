**Abstract**

The framework establishes what is good and that conscious agents are obligated toward it. This paper asks the next question: what, then, do we do? It maps the strategic repertoire for two kinds of work — strengthening and building Emergent systems (generative connection) and dissolving the structures that foreclose emergence (generative separation) — and supplies a diagnosis stage that matches a system's profile to its appropriate treatment. Both kinds of work are generative; the actor sets out to do no degenerative work, and there is no degenerative mandate.

The paper's central structural claim is that pathological systems — Rigid and Destructive — cannot reliably self-correct, because the property that defines them is the suppression or foreclosure of the very diversity that would permit correction. Change therefore requires a perturbation the frozen core cannot dissipate, together with a viable alternative attractor for the freed substrate to migrate into. A consequence runs through every strategy below: a better story changes nothing on its own. What migrates a system is a relational environment that carries a story — never a story by itself. Offered without a real new relationship and a real basin to inhabit, a better story backfires, proving the new story unreliable and re-entrenching the old one.

The repertoire is governed throughout by the Operating Charter. Applications to specific domains — markets, governance, regional conflict — are held for a companion applications paper, where each case can be handled through the diagnosis stage and the Charter rather than asserted in the abstract.

## I. What This Paper Is Doing

The core document and its companions are diagnostic: they describe what existence is, what goodness is, and why agents are obligated toward generative emergence. This paper is the turn from diagnosis to action — the *so what do we do* once the obligation is accepted.

There are two mandates, and they correspond to the framework's two generative quadrants:

- **Generative connection (Emergent):** build new Emergent systems and strengthen the emergent capacity of existing ones.
- **Generative separation (Protective):** dissolve, contain, or reroute the coordinating structures that foreclose emergence — without foreclosing anything in turn.

Both are generative. The actor never sets out to occupy the Destructive quad. Where genuine destruction appears at all, it appears only inside protective work, in a gated tier whose execution the Operating Charter places outside any private actor's authority (§VIII below; Charter §III).

This paper describes the *full* repertoire, including that gated tier, because a usable manual must map the whole terrain. Which parts of the terrain a given actor may walk is fixed by the Charter, not by this paper.

## II. The Two Things That Hold a System Together

A conscious agent's reactive orientation — what triggers its emotions, which relationships it forms in each direction, which quadrant it occupies — is held in place by two things: a **model** built from relational experience, and a **story** the model tells about itself. *(This applies in full to the reactive, quad-choosing layer of the self; it is not a claim that the whole self is a story. The self also has a body and a minimal, non-narrative center that persists when the narrative layer goes quiet — which is precisely what makes a story-migration survivable. See* Consciousness as Ecosystem *§II;* The Emotional Map *§III–VI.)*

The relationship between the two is asymmetric. The story is mostly the **readout** of the relational model — the Interpreter's gloss on a structure built through embodied experience — and only occasionally a **lever** back into it. This is why the model is resistant to purely cognitive correction: you cannot think your way out of a model built through relational experience (*The Emotional Map* §V). A story changed while the relational substrate stays fixed is a resolution the system does not actually run.

The same structure holds at the collective scale. A society or culture is bound by a shared myth riding on a web of relational and ritual bonds. The myth is not believed first and then enacted; the binding is produced first — through synchronized ritual, shared sound, the felt dissolution of individual into group — and the narrative is injected into the bound state, where it is received not as a stranger's claim to be evaluated but as something the group already knows (*Sound Before Culture* and *The Carrier Wave*: binding first, narrative second).

**The operative implication for every strategy below:** you cannot move a system by offering it a better story. The sequence that actually migrates a system is: an emerging sense of kinship and trust → the loosening of the old narrative → the offer of a new narrative → and, indispensably, a real new relationship and a real attractor basin to inhabit. Remove the last term and the attempt backfires.

**The nesting principle.** There is a second, distinct way the attempt backfires, independent of the relational container: when the new story *negates* the old one. "Your past identity is false; here is what is really going on" is received as an attack on the self, maximizes cognitive dissonance, and triggers defense — the old story wins by reflex. The migration that succeeds does the opposite: it **contains** the prior identity rather than dissolving it. "Your story is real; there is a larger story that includes it and unites us, and that does not dissolve who you are." The prior identity is nested, intact and differentiated, inside a wider frame.

This is not merely a softer tactic. It is the framework's own connection rule applied to identity. Generative connection preserves the differentiation of what it connects; connection that dissolves its parts into an undifferentiated whole is the Rigid failure mode (Core §6.3, §9.2). A switcheroo that erases the prior identity is therefore itself a *degenerate* move — assimilation, not emergence — and it reproduces at the level of identity the very pathology the intervention claims to cure. A switcheroo that nests the prior identity inside a larger differentiated frame is generative connection by definition. The goodness criterion — richer connectedness that *preserves differentiation* — is satisfied or violated by the migration itself, not only by its destination. *(The move has a long pedigree: Hegelian sublation, in which a stage is preserved-and-superseded rather than annihilated; inclusivist rather than exclusivist religious conversion; narrative therapy's externalizing of the problem so the person's identity is reframed as separate from the pathology rather than indicted by it. Interpretive.)*

## III. Why Pathological Systems Cannot Reliably Self-Correct

A **Rigid** system maintains coherence by suppressing internal diversity; in dynamical terms it occupies the near-frozen ordered regime, in which perturbations die out rather than generating new structure (Core §9.2). By construction it dissipates the internal variation that would let it correct. Correction therefore requires a perturbation the frozen core cannot absorb.

But *external* overstates the requirement. The corrective agent need only originate outside the **frozen core**, not outside the system's boundary. Suppressed-but-not-eliminated internal diversity — a reformist faction, a dissident, the part of an individual self that never fully converted — is the cheapest available lever, because the kinship and trust it needs are already present inside the target. The Reformation came from within Christendom; glasnost from within the Politburo. *(Interpretive.)*

This yields a diagnostic with real predictive content — **the belief-gap metric.** The gap between *professed* and *felt* belief in a system's substrate predicts its brittleness:

- **Imposed rigidity** — coherence held by force — leaves a resentful substrate, compliance without conviction, a wide gap, a fragile system with abundant exploitable internal diversity.
- **Internalized rigidity** — coherence held by a genuinely believed sacred myth, with identity fused to the collective — leaves almost no gap; the suppressed diversity is thin and the system is correspondingly expensive to move. Sacred values are nonfungible and resist material trade-offs; offering incentives to abandon them can *backfire*, hardening refusal (Atran and Ginges, on devoted actors).

The gap is observable, not merely conceptual: defection rates when the stick weakens, black markets, samizdat, emigration spikes when borders open.

A **Destructive** system is organized around foreclosing external systems. It is ultimately self-limiting — it consumes the adaptive capacity of its hosts and eventually its own substrate — but it inflicts catastrophic, often irreversible harm on the way down (Core §9.7). Against it, containment, the acceleration of its built-in collapse, and the migration of its substrate usually dominate force.

## IV. The Diagnosis Stage

No strategy is selected before the target is read. Each unique profile has its matched antidote; the organizing principle of the manual is that you identify the relationship and the myth that bind a system, and the specific pathology yields its specific treatment. The reading has six dimensions:

1. **Quad profile, per axis** — internal, lateral, downward, upward; item-specific, so each axis is a map, not a single value (Core §10).
2. **Propagation mode** — contained or viral (Core §9.6). Contained pathology may be left to quarantine; viral pathology demands active response.
3. **Rigidity type** — imposed or internalized, read through the belief-gap.
4. **The binding myth** — what story holds the system, where it came from, and what genuine need it meets. (A pathology is almost always a real drive misrouted, not a different motivation; *The Emotional Map* §V.)
5. **The substrate/coordination map** — which parts are people (substrate, immune under Charter §II.1) and which are coordinating structure (the legitimate object of separation).
6. **Power and authority context** — relative power, and whether any legitimate collective authority is in play. Plus the **window state**: is the system in a stable phase or a liminal, plastic one?

### The Signature of Emergence: A Diagnostic Instrument

Before the formal six-dimension reading, a system's quadrant usually shows on its surface. Three indicators, read together, give a fast and falsifiable signature of Emergent Alignment — a second observable instrument alongside the belief-gap metric of §III, and one the framework needs in order to make claims that can be checked rather than merely asserted (the unfalsifiability risk surfaced in the case program).

**Indicator 1 — Distributed abundance.** A genuinely Emergent system generates surplus value, *and that value flows down to its subsystems*. The italicized clause carries the whole indicator. Abundance alone reads nothing: a system can be enormously abundant at the top while extracting from its parts — a monopoly is abundant, a parasitic empire is abundant. What distinguishes Emergent abundance from extractive abundance is the *downward axis* — whether the parts are richer for belonging. This is the hardest indicator to fake, because variety can be staged and harmony can be enforced, but you cannot easily counterfeit surplus actually reaching the subsystems. It is therefore the load-bearing indicator of the three.

**Indicator 2 — Endogenous diversity.** Emergent systems are diverse: many kinds of thing, many positions, dissent that is tolerated and metabolized into robustness (Core §6.3). But diversity reads the quad *only if its provenance is checked*. Diversity that arose from the system's own dynamics is a true Emergent signal. Diversity that was *installed* from above — mandated, quota-met, staged — is often a Rigid system wearing Emergent clothing, and reads the opposite of what it appears to. The diagnostic question is therefore never "is there diversity?" but "**where did this diversity come from?**" Endogenous diversity is the symptom of health; installed diversity is frequently a symptom of the disease.

**Indicator 3 — Generative tension.** Tension is a dial, not a switch, and its *quality* reads the quad. Too little friction — enforced agreement, brittle calm — reads Rigid (the frozen, perturbation-dissipating regime). Destructive friction — hostile, foreclosing conflict that consumes the parts — reads Destructive. Friction that *generates* — playful, productive disagreement, rivalrous cooperation, debate that sharpens rather than eliminates — is the criticality signature of a system poised at the edge between rigid order and chaos, which is where emergence lives. So the diagnostic is not whether a system has conflict but what its conflict *does*.

**The governing correction: these are markers, not mechanisms.** All three are *outputs* that indicate a system is doing the right things — visible traces of genuine connection that preserves differentiation. None is an *input* you can install to produce emergence. This matters most for diversity, where the error is most common and most consequential: enforcing diversity top-down mistakes the readout for the lever. It is itself the Rigid move — a higher level dictating a required configuration to the level below — and it produces the pathologies of imposed rigidity (tokenism, resentment, brittle compliance, a wide belief-gap), not emergence. The generative path is never to install the outputs; it is to build the conditions under which genuine connection occurs, after which the abundance, diversity, and generative tension appear on their own, as symptoms of health rather than causes of it. The causal arrow runs connection → distributed abundance and endogenous diversity → robustness — never diversity → robustness directly. *(This is a structural claim about causal direction, distinct from any political slogan for or against diversity: the framework holds that healthy emergence generates diversity and that diversity cannot be reverse-engineered into a system by fiat. The slogan "diversity is our strength" is half-right — Emergent systems are both strong and diverse — and half-wrong about the mechanism, since the strength is not obtained by injecting the diversity.)*

**As a measurement program.** Because the three indicators are observable, systems can be scored on them — distributed abundance, the provenance and breadth of diversity, the quality of tension — and the framework then makes checkable predictions: a high-signature system should show greater robustness, adaptability, and durable legitimacy than a low-signature one (Core §6.5). The case program bears this out where it has been run: the Achaemenid signature was high on all three and the Assyrian low, and the durability-of-legitimacy outcomes followed; the diverse gut flora outperformed the monoculture left by ablation. Turning the three indicators into a usable score — and finding the cases where a high-signature system was *not* more robust — is flagged as open work.

### Substrate-Fitting: Form Follows the Components

The diagnosis reads the system as it is; this principle governs what may be built or changed from there. **The reachable, stable higher-order form is a function of the components' capacity-profile.** You cannot impose an arbitrary structure on a substrate and expect it to hold — a regulator needs variety matching what it regulates (Ashby), and emergence is substrate-conditioned all the way down. A structure fitted to its components is enabling scaffolding; one imposed against their grain is Rigid, and Rigid fractures. The "it won't work, it will only generate contradiction" intuition is exactly this: the imposed form and the substrate's actual dynamics pull apart.

This is the system-scale twin of fitness-localness (*Individual Worth* §8): there, deploy each node by its fitness; here, compose each system by its components' profile. It adds a question the six-dimension reading does not yet ask — *what is the capacity-profile of the substrate, and what forms can it sustain?* — and it is why no play in this manual is cut-and-paste. A self-selected population of exit-seekers sustains a different constitution than a stayed-in-place continuity; neither could wear the other's. Every intervention — composing a new system, modifying an existing one, or separating one for protection — is substrate-relative, and the understanding must come before the move.

It also explains the framework's pluralism. Two things are universal: the **floor** (every substrate) and, for wager-takers, the **direction** (generative emergence — a heading, not a blueprint). *Form* is never prescribed; it is always substrate-fitted and plural, at every tier. The vast legitimate variety of the non-destructive middle is precisely this — the space of substrate-fitted forms, the readout of substrate-variety.

Three guards keep the principle from becoming apologetics:

1. **It governs form above the floor — never the floor.** "Our people need a strong hand / are not suited to freedom" is the signature move of Rigid imposition *dressed as* fitting, and usually false, because the repression runs against the substrate's differentiation rather than with it. The principle never certifies that a system holds the floor; "it fits our people" is the floor-breacher's alibi. Form is substrate-relative; the floor is not.
2. **Profile is current and malleable, not an essence.** Systems select and then shape their own substrate, and profiles change — sometimes radically — within a few generations. Treating profile as fixed essence yields "some peoples are unfit for freedom," the essentialist engine of every civilizational hierarchy. Fit the *current* profile while also shifting it.
3. **A substrate-fitted form can still contain a floor-breach.** The clearest case is a liberty-form built for self-selected freedom-seekers that coexisted, for generations, with chattel slavery for the people its founding story omitted. The fit was real; it did not excuse the breach. The floor-firewall travels with the principle wherever it goes.

### Coordination Forms and the Capture Signature: Two Diagnostic Instruments

Substrate-fitting establishes that the legitimate middle is a space of many forms rather than one. Two descriptive instruments sharpen the reading of that space: a typology of the forms collective coordination actually takes, and a signature that separates a generative collective from a captured one. Both feed the diagnosis only. Neither carries a mandate, and the floor, the exit-ethic (§XII), and the Charter govern whatever is done with them. They are lifted as instruments from an adjacent information-theoretic framework whose own influence-cultivation program this paper does not adopt; the primary sources are cited directly, so that the lineage is owned rather than the aggregator.

**Four forms of coordination (TIMN).** Ronfeldt's framework (Arquilla and Ronfeldt, RAND) reduces collective organization to four forms that layer rather than replace one another: the kinship-based **Tribe**, bound by lineage and direct relation; the hierarchical **Institution**, bound by delegated authority (the army, the church, the bureaucratic state); the **Market**, bound by exchange and price; and the **Network**, bound by lateral, web-like ties. Its load-bearing claim is developmental: a society grows more capable as it adds forms, and a mature one must hold all four in balance. This is the system-scale restatement of the framework's own diversity test (*Signature of Emergence*, above): the higher-order system becomes more robust as the diversity of lower-order coordination forms rises. The pathology to read for is the monoform. The pure Institution is the Rigid state; the pure Market dissolves the commons into atomized exchange; the pure Tribe is the closed clan; the pure Network is the un-anchored swarm. Each form, universalized over the others, forecloses them, which is the monoculture failure (Core §6.3) scoped to the level where coordination occurs. TIMN supplies a concrete vocabulary for the substrate-fitted variety the previous section insists on, and a named target for re-diversification (Play 2): restore the suppressed *form*, not merely the suppressed content.

**The capture signature.** A collective that coordinates behavior and polices its boundary is not pathological by that fact alone (§XII). The diagnostic questions are *how it binds* and *at what cost one leaves*. Three observable markers separate a generative plur from a captured one.

- *Binding fuel.* Generative binding runs through truthful relation and a real shared good; captured binding runs through managed emotion. The egregore literature (Stavish) states the mechanism in its own idiom: emotional arousal is the entity's fuel. In the framework's terms this is the §XII correction restated at the level of formation. Emotion and morale are an output of reciprocal contribution, never a lever, and a collective that pulls the emotional lever to manufacture cohesion is already drifting toward capture.
- *Exit cost.* Accounts of leaving high-cohesion groups report that exit is painful and frequently requires outside support, not because visible walls are present but because the binding has been internalized. This is the empirical face of the *de-facto low-exit* category (§XII): the wall one cannot see because it is inside. The signature is never "does it cohere" but "is exit cheap."
- *Narrative monopoly.* "Controlling the narrative" (the netwar formulation) names the move from a story offered to a story enforced, and the closing of the revisability that re-diversification restores. A generative collective holds its story as revisable and survives deviation; a captured one makes the story unfalsifiable and polices it.

Read together, the typology says what shape a system has taken; the signature says whether that shape holds its members through value or through capture. The first feeds substrate-fitting and re-diversification; the second feeds the exit diagnostic (§XII) and the protective-separation warrant (§VIII).

### The Predatory Emergent: Failure Paths That Pass the Internal Look

This is the instrument the other three in this section exist to serve, and it is a standing warning rather than a closed catalogue. The paths named below are the ones the framework has found so far. They are not the whole set, and a reader who memorizes them and stops has already failed the instruction. What must be internalized is not the list but the signature every entry shares: a system can run a genuine emergent relation among its own parts while its net effect on the layer beneath it and the systems around it is degenerative. Read from inside, such a system passes every local test of Emergent Alignment. Its differentiation is preserved, its cooperation is real, its higher-order coherence is produced. The failure is not visible at internal scope at all. It becomes visible only on the downward and outward axes, and only when those axes are actually checked.

The scope distinction is what keeps this consistent with the Signature of Emergence instrument above. That instrument is right that a parasitic empire is abundant, not Emergent, and the verdict it issues is the whole-system verdict. This section adds only that the same system can carry a real emergent relation at internal scope, and that the internal reality is what makes the pattern hard to see and easy to defend. Internal emergence is not a mitigation of the external harm. It is the camouflage for it. A system is never cleared by being genuinely generative somewhere inside itself. It is cleared only by its cross-layer effect, and the two come apart routinely.

Turn the instrument on your own project before turning it outward. This is the Parasitization Problem addressed to the person holding the framework. A functioning emergent system is the richest thing in its environment to capture, and its own participants are the least equipped to notice the capture, because from where they stand the abundance and the cooperation are both real. The wager holder is not exempt and is in fact the prime target. Run the test on the downward and outward axes, not on the internal relation that will always report health.

The test is stated once and not repeated per face. Do not certify a system from its internal relation. Judge what reaches the layer below and the systems outside. The three markers that carry the test are already given above in the Signature of Emergence: whether surplus flows down to the subsystems, whether the coordination shows the capture signature, and whether low exit reflects genuine value or manufactured internalization. Those markers do the work. This section insists only that they be run downward and outward rather than across the internal relation alone.

The known faces follow. Each is an instance of the one failure, not a separate mechanism, and each is given with the marker that exposes it.

**The Bloater.** The system builds a real generative environment and harvests its surplus into a single node, where the surplus is sequestered rather than recirculated. Sequestered capacity performs no generative work by definition, so it becomes a standing maintenance cost, and that cost forces the acquisition of the next host to fund it. The accumulation is not stored value. It is a liability that drives serial re-hosting, and it can be redirected to subsidize degenerate expansion elsewhere. Marker: abundance concentrates upward and stops there, and the parts are not richer for belonging.

**The Author.** The system holds genuine power to author new forms and uses it to generate variety while overriding the standing of what it authors. Formal variety is maximized and the agency of the authored elements is reduced to nothing. This is the error of mistaking differentiation of form for differentiation of standing. The framework requires the second and not merely the first, so a system that produces rich variety from components whose standing it has overridden has committed a floor violation wearing the appearance of emergence. Marker: the diversity is real and the components cannot refuse their role. The limit case is the alien intelligence of Kosemen's speculative work *All Tomorrows*, which breeds a descendant species into many elaborate forms of suffering.

**The Coercive Gardener.** The system forces generative conditions by destroying whatever substrate it judges not to belong, over-engineering the ecosystem instead of working with its components. Two failures are braided here. Mis-aimed excision is Destructive misidentification, in which the targeting has degraded and the intervention removes generative structure. Over-control is the Rigid move, in which mandating the outputs of health from above forecloses the substrate's own capacity to produce them. Both wear the intention of cultivating emergence. Marker: the gardener installs the symptoms of health rather than building the conditions for them, and the substrate's own agency contracts under the intervention.

**The Potemkin.** The system enforces the appearance of emergence by staging internal differentiation and displaying abundance without engaging the real nature of the substrate. Because the substrate is not actually self-organizing into the displayed order, the appearance has to be held together by continuous energy injection, and the maintenance cost stays high or rises rather than falling. The discriminator here is untested, not falsified: genuine emergence tends to lower the energy required to hold coherence over time, because the parts are self-maintaining and remain by their own tendency, whereas staged emergence pays an ongoing coercion cost to keep the display from reverting. Marker: coherence that costs more to maintain than it returns, and that reverts the moment the energy stops.

The catalogue ends here for now and is expected to grow. A new face will not arrive labeled as a new mechanism. It will present as a functioning emergent system, and it will be caught, if it is caught at all, by the same test: not the internal relation, but the effect below and outside.

## V. Generative-Connection Plays (Building)

**1. Attractor-Building.**
Construct the alternative basin a system or person can choose to migrate into. This is the first obligation, because every other play depends on there being somewhere to go. An attempt to dissolve a pathology without a built alternative produces a vacuum, and vacuums are captured by whatever attractor is most ready — usually the worst one.

A built basin is not yet a stable one. Held only as shared story, a meme, it stays revisable and fragile; it stabilizes when it crystallizes into durable form: the tools, institutions, texts, and law that Blackmore's third replicator class names the *teme*. The build obligation therefore runs in two stages. Construct the basin, then crystallize it into temes that carry it past the founders' attention. An attractor that never hardens past memetic circulation will lose to one that has (see *Coordination Forms and the Capture Signature*, §IV).

**2. Re-diversification.**
Restore suppressed differentiation to a Rigid system, bringing it back toward Emergent Alignment (Core §6.3): reintroduce the variation it eliminated, and relax the top-down over-specification that demands a single permitted configuration. The diagnostic for success is the framework's own test — does the higher-level system become *more* robust as lower-level diversity increases?

**3. Container-Carried Switcheroo.**
The migration mechanism itself. Build the relational environment first — kinship and trust — then loosen the old narrative, offer the new one, and back it with a real new relationship and a real basin to inhabit. The narrative is the payload; the relational container is the mechanism. Two failure modes to avoid, both treated in §II: a story delivered without the container does not update the model and backfires; and a story that *negates* the prior identity rather than **nesting** it triggers identity-defense and backfires. The successful form preserves the differentiation of the prior identity inside a larger frame that contains it — generative connection applied to the self.

*(On forced assimilation that eventually works — Jacobin France: the framework does not claim negation never produces a durable outcome. Jacobin suppression of Breton and Occitan eventually produced a durable, genuinely internalized French national identity. The framework's claim is that nesting is structurally more reliable and more generative, and that the moral cost of forced assimilation is the violation of agency during the coercive phase — regardless of whether the outcome proves durable. Where forced assimilation does produce lasting cohesion, the mechanism is the eventual internalization, not the coercion. The coercion was the vehicle; the internalization was what made it stick. The nesting strategy achieves the same end without the agency cost.)*

## VI. Generative-Separation Plays (Non-Coercive)

**4. Internal Fracture.**
Find, protect, and amplify the system's own suppressed dissent. This is the cheapest and most elegant separation available, because the kinship and trust it requires already exist inside the target; the dissenting element only needs help carrying the burden of revising the myth. *Precondition:* the fractured element must be **survivable**. Where a coordinating apparatus murders its own dissenters — a viral-Rigid structure defending itself by pre-emptively eliminating its internal diversity — the prerequisite move is to build protected **exterior** space (exit, diaspora, external platforms) where the suppressed element can stay alive long enough to carry a new myth. This is why reform movements so often incubate in exile.

**5. Containment.**
The dampening tier: deny propagation, reduce amplitude, buy time, and build immune structure around the pathology. Non-coercive forms — exposure, transparency, deplatforming within legitimate norms, and building competing institutions that simply outcompete the pathological one — are inside a private actor's envelope. Coercive forms — imprisonment, quarantine by force — require legitimate collective authority (Charter §II.5).

**6. Window Timing.**
A timing modifier on every play above. Liminal windows — crisis, rupture, transition, the loosening of a social prior under shock — make myths revisable for a period. **Manufacturing the window** — deliberately destabilizing a system to create plasticity — is the highest-risk strategy in the repertoire and in most cases self-defeating (the destabilization trap): the window is captured by whatever attractor is most ready, which is equally available to demagogues, as Weimar demonstrates. The absolute claim "never manufacture" is disconfirmed by cases where manufactured rupture produced durable generative outcomes — the American Revolution, the Meiji Restoration — but those successes required three conditions: a ready constructive attractor already existed before the rupture; the destabilization targeted a coordinating structure rather than a substrate; and the mandate was genuine emergence rather than mere destruction of the prior order. Manufacturing without these conditions is degenerate; manufacturing with them is not categorically prohibited but remains the highest-risk play in the kit, demanding the most rigorous gate. The operative default is therefore constructive: **build the trusted alternative attractor before the window opens**, so the healthy basin is the available one when entropy spikes naturally.

## VII. Entropy Injection / Myth Destabilization — The High-Gate Tool

The deliberate loosening of a Rigid system's canalized myth so it becomes revisable — the social analog of the psychedelic. It is the most dangerous tool in the kit, for three reasons. It *manufactures* the plasticity window rather than using a natural one. In its disorienting or false form it is indistinguishable from disinformation and active measures. And a myth dissolved with no ready replacement produces anomie, which the worst-ready attractor captures.

Its gate is correspondingly heavy. It is legitimate only when the target is genuinely Rigid or Destructive (never an Emergent system — flooding goodness with doubt merely degrades it), a ready constructive attractor already exists for the freed substrate, and **the means are truthful**: true disconfirming information and real alternative connection, never manufactured falsehood. The truthful version largely collapses back into Re-diversification (Play 2) and the Container-Carried Switcheroo (Play 3). The false, disorienting version is **corruption** — forbidden outright by the Operating Charter (§II.4), because it manufactures in the substrate the very Rigidity the intervention claimed to cure. This tool is retained in the theory and placed outside the actor's envelope in any coercive or deceptive form.

## VIII. The Locked Drawer: Gated Force

Documented for completeness; its execution is placed outside any private actor's authority by the Operating Charter (§III). Genuine destruction — degenerative separation deployed protectively — occurs only in the supreme-emergency case the just-war tradition recognizes (*Conflict, Suffering* §6). The four warrant tests that define its boundary are stated in full in Charter §III and summarized here:

> active harm, never strength of conviction, is the warrant; the honest form of the warrant is a constraint on the actor's options, not a verdict on the target's irredeemability; destruction targets coordination and frees substrate, with the continuous alarm *am I destroying the structure to free the substrate, or the substrate to eliminate the structure?*; and weakness can license defensive separation while power obligates reintegration — the genocidal signature being a powerful actor using existential-threat rhetoric to license offensive destruction of a substrate.

The biology supplies a literal instance of *power obligates reintegration*. Michael Levin's work frames cancer as a cell's loss of bioelectric communication with the collective — the cellular "self" contracting to a single-cell scope — and pursues **renormalization**: restoring the defecting cell to the collective pattern rather than killing it (Levin 2021, *Prog. Biophys. Mol. Biol.* 165:102–113). Reintegration of the defecting node over its elimination, demonstrated at the tissue scale — the moral-default ordering running in the substrate itself. One honest qualifier: more connection is not always normalizing; the right bioelectric *topology* is what matters, which is differentiation-preserving connection, not fusion.

## IX. The Repertoire as a Whole

| Target profile | Primary plays |
|---|---|
| Emergent, contained | Leave be; protect; learn from |
| Emergent, viral | Support; connect; amplify |
| Rigid, imposed (wide belief-gap) | Internal Fracture; Re-diversification; Container-Carried Switcheroo |
| Rigid, internalized (narrow belief-gap) | Attractor-Building first; patient Container-Carried Switcheroo; Window Timing; symbolic (never material) engagement with sacred values |
| Destructive, contained | Containment; quarantine via legitimate authority |
| Destructive, viral | Containment + Attractor-Building for the substrate + (gated, legitimate-authority-only) force against coordination |

Three notes govern the table. **The moral default ordering** runs from connection plays and Internal Fracture (cheapest, most reversible, most agency-preserving) through containment to entropy injection and force (most dangerous, most gated, mostly outside the actor's hands). **Strategies braid** — most real interventions combine several, and the table names emphases, not exclusive assignments. And the **governing asymmetry** holds throughout: the build half is available to any actor on its own authority; the coercive part of the separation half is reserved to legitimate collective authority (Charter §IV).

## X. Starting Points: What This Asks of You

The repertoire above is written for the strategist acting on systems. But the turn to action does not wait for anyone to become a strategist.

The framework does not command anyone. It offers a wager: that orienting your agency toward generative emergence is worth doing. You can see the direction and decline to walk it — that stays a real choice. But if you take the bet, acting is not an order from outside; it is what your own wager commits you to, and the responsibility it carries scales with how clearly you have come to see (Core §16: higher consciousness raises responsibility, not entitlement).

The method isn't fixed either. Everything below is a starting hypothesis — the framework's first guesses about what acting on the wager looks like, to be tested in real life and revised by what the testing shows. The goal is a direction, never a finished state; the methods are means, and means are meant to vary.

Four entry points, ordered as the rest of the manual is — from the cheapest and most reversible to the most dangerous and most gated.

**Reflect — turn the lens on yourself first.** Before acting on any system you do not govern, use the framework where the risk is lowest and the test most honest: on your own life. Map your quad profile, your relationships, your family, your work, the institutions you belong to. Does it clarify them — make the dynamics legible, give you coherence and something to actually do — or doesn't it? This is the cheapest move, the safest, and the one place you can check the framework against direct experience rather than take it on faith. It is also the precondition for the rest: an agent who has not seen clearly, himself included, has not earned the standing to act on others. (Diagnosis stage, §IV.)

**Build — make new things that are basins in their own right.** The first obligation of generative connection is that there be somewhere good to go (Attractor-Building, §V.1). So build: products, institutions, communities, art, research — systems that act in the world and draw people in by being better to inhabit, not by argument. A built alternative that simply outcompetes the pathological option does more than any critique of it. This is open to any agent on its own authority (Charter §IV); you need no one's permission to build something good.

**Improve — raise the emergent capacity of what already exists.** Most of the world is not yours to build from scratch; it is existing systems — markets, networks, teams, institutions — that are less emergent than they could be. Improve them: restore suppressed differentiation, relax the over-specification that demands a single permitted configuration, build the conditions under which genuine connection occurs so that abundance, diversity, and generative tension appear on their own (§IV; Re-diversification, §V.2). The discipline is the framework's own warning: build the conditions, never install the outputs. Mandating the symptoms of health from above is itself the Rigid move.

**Protect — identify what forecloses, and meet it without becoming it.** Learn to read threats — personal and psychological as much as social: the manipulative relationship, the Rigid dynamic hardening in a family or a workplace, the Destructive coordination in the wider world. Then act, within a strict envelope. Against personal threats: boundaries, exit, exposure, refusing to carry a myth that isn't yours. Against larger ones: the non-coercive separation plays — protect the vulnerable substrate, amplify suppressed dissent, contain by transparency and by building what outcompetes (§VI). The line that makes this protection rather than aggression is the Charter's: you act on structures, incentives, and coordination, never on persons, and coercive force is not yours to wield — it belongs to legitimate collective authority (Charter §II.1, §IV). The grammar of protection is, before the fact, identical to the grammar of aggression. The guardrails are what hold them apart, and they are not optional.

None of this is final. We intend to keep developing the framework for as long as it proves useful, and to revise or abandon what doesn't — applying to the framework itself the test it applies to everything else, and refusing the one move it most forbids: treating itself as finished. And we spread it the only way consistent with what it says — not by imposing it, but by building basins good enough that people choose to enter them. Goodness propagates by attraction, or it is not goodness propagating. The rest is found out by doing.

## XI. The Interference Warrant — A First Treatment

The repertoire above answers *how* to act on a system. It does not yet answer *when* acting on a system you do not govern is warranted rather than merely strategic — the question flagged below (North Korea's internal suppression with no external-expansion threat; intervention against an atrocity by a power with no territorial ambition). A first structural pass, drawn from *Individual Worth* §7:

**1. The floor is the first gate, and it is universal.** No interference may breach the floor over persons; and — the symmetric point — active harm to persons *inside* a system is a real wrong whether or not that system threatens anyone outside it. The floor does not wait for an expansion trigger. So the "but they weren't expanding" defense (North Korea; a non-expansionist génocidaire) is a non-answer to the *warrant* question: internal atrocity warrants response on its own.

**2. But warrant-for-response is not warrant-for-force.** These are different bars, and conflating them is the error under most of the debate.
- The **build and non-coercive-separation half** of the repertoire (support suppressed dissent, build exit and exterior platforms, expose, outcompete, contain within legitimate norms) is warranted by internal active harm to persons *as such*, on any actor's own authority (the Charter asymmetry). No expansion trigger and no supreme-emergency is required to justify *these*.
- **Gated force** (degenerative separation, §VIII) keeps its full supreme-emergency bar — the four warrant tests, *active harm not conviction*, and *power obligates reintegration*. The expansion / supreme-emergency condition is a gate on **force**, not on interference in general.

So the North Korea and non-expansionist-atrocity cases resolve, at first pass, as: the non-force repertoire is warranted by the internal harm now; force remains behind the §VIII gate.

**3. The warrant scales with the harm the response inflicts** (evidence-scaled severity, *Individual Worth* §7.5): the more destructive the intervention, the higher the burden of *demonstrated active harm* — never strength of disapproval. Against the threat only (distinction); beneath the floor as cap (no atrocity, no targeting identity-as-such, no forcing conscience, at any proof level).

**4. Conduct, not belief.** Interference is warranted against active harm and the coordinating apparatus producing it — never against belief or ascriptive identity as such (the firewall already in §VIII: *active harm, never strength of conviction*).

**5. Choosing the quad of response.** For any diagnosis of the form "this agent/system could do better and is failing or harming," the response is not single — it splits into the four quads, and the moral-default ordering (§IX) governs the choice. Worked on the live "those with great capacity who do not relieve a remediable harm are culpable" claim:
- *Destructive* — seize, liquidate, or destroy the capable agent: breakdown without regeneration; usually destroys the productive structure and fails to transfer the capacity as usable relief.
- *Rigid* — compel the capacity by uniform top-down law, choice suppressed: coheres, but suppresses the generative incentive that produced the capacity and is brittle to perturbation.
- *Protective* — dissolve the degenerative configurations that actually produce the harm (the corruption, conflict, and distribution failures — *Sen*, *Poverty and Famines*), not the capacity itself; bounded by the floor and this warrant.
- *Emergent* — those with the fitness to build construct the institutions and cooperation that can absorb and direct the capacity toward proven root-solutions, and invite the capable to fund them. The obligation is *relocated by fitness*: it falls on whoever can build the absorbing-systems, not only on whoever holds the resource (whose fitness may not match the saving-work). Critique is the system's feedback channel; the generative move is critique *plus* building, never the silencing of either.

**6. The scope guard.** None of this licenses a node to arrogate the god's-eye optimization of the system. Interference orients *your own contribution* under the Charter asymmetry; the telos is a direction navigated by structural reading, not a destination computed and imposed (*Individual Worth* §7.0, §7.3). The actor stays scoped far narrower than the theory.

This is a first treatment, not the dedicated account the question still deserves: it fixes the floor as universal, separates warrant-for-response from warrant-for-force, scales by evidence, holds to conduct, and routes the response through the quads — leaving the harder residue (the threshold of "demonstrated active harm," cross-sovereignty tradeoffs, the cross-tier cases) for the full *Applications* treatment.

## XII. Exit, Expulsion, and Membership Obligation

A consolidated system raises three questions about its boundary: who may leave, who may be removed, and what those who stay owe. They look separate and are one structure. All three are governed by the same three instruments — the floor, the conduct/conscience line (*The Emotional Map* §VI: force reaches bodies, never the choice-realm), and the quad-test — and their legitimacy moves together with a single variable: **the voluntariness of membership.**

**Exit — held by attraction, not walls.** "Cannot exit" does three different jobs. *Physical impossibility*: the rock in the gravity well, the cell with nowhere to go but death. *De-jure prevention*: exit is possible but forbidden and punished — the cage, the closed border, the community that kills apostates. *De-facto low exit*: the door is open, exit is available, and few take it because what they would leave is good. A healthy consolidated attractor is the third. Its depth is *how good it is to be in*, not *how punished you are for leaving*; the diagnostic is never "do people leave?" but "*can* they?" North Korea and a flourishing open society can share an emigration rate and be structural opposites. One sharpening the capture literature forces (§IV): the open door with few leavers is not self-certifying. Internalized capture also produces an open door that few use, because the cost of leaving was built into the member through managed emotion rather than earned through a real good. The third case therefore splits in two. Depth by genuine value and depth by manufactured internalization look identical at the level of exit rate, and the binding-fuel marker is what separates them. The diagnostic refines accordingly: not "can they exit yet stay," but "is what holds them a real good or a built compulsion."

The disanalogy with a body is the load-bearing point. A cell cannot exit to a better life — outside is death, no alternative basin. A *generative* super-system is the one thing a body is not: internally plural and reconfigurable (§IV, substrate-fitting). Exit inside it is not exit into the void; it is mobility among sub-basins — leave this faith, nation, community, join another — and, deepest, the right to *form a new one*. The test of whether a consolidated system stayed generative or curdled into a cage: can you still move between sub-cultures, and still build a new one? A Rigid system forbids new formations; a generative one enables them (the Emergent quad). Only two things are truly non-exitable: the **floor** (a protection, not a cage — you may always decline the wager *down* to the neutral middle; only floor-breaching meets force) and the **wager-direction** (invited, never forced — coercing it is the §VI category error). And there is a standing danger: success *tempts* exit-closing, self-defense sliding into the viral-Rigid drift. So exitability is not a founding condition that lapses once the basin is deep — it is the permanent guard, more load-bearing at consolidation, not less.

**Expulsion — the protective-separation play, under guardrails.** A community may, and must, police its own membership boundary; a community that cannot draw one is not a differentiated node but fusion. Expulsion from *belonging or practice* is legitimate boundary-keeping. It is the protective-separation machinery (§VIII; Charter §III) applied to membership, and the death camp is precisely what remains when its guardrails are stripped, in order:

- *Floor cap.* Remove membership or liberty; never the floor. Prison keeps rights and standing; the camp removes them. The historical catastrophe is the slide from "you are not one of us" (herem — legitimate) to "and therefore you may be harmed" (pogrom — floor-breach).
- *Conduct, not identity.* Expel for what someone *did* (individualized, desert-based), never for what they *are* (collective, category-based). That single switch is most of the distance between prison and camp.
- *Due process + evidence-scaled severity* (§XI). Category-expulsion skips exactly this.
- *Protective, not Rigid* — the continuous alarm: am I separating to free the substrate, or to eliminate it? Expelling a real destroyer is protective; expelling dissent or difference for uniformity is the viral-Rigid move wearing boundary-keeping's clothes.
- *Reversibility + a door back.* Prefer quarantine, rehabilitation, reintegration over the irreversible; power obligates the least-destructive option (Levin, §VIII).

Symmetry holds: forced-stay and forced-expulsion are both coercive. Healthy belonging is voluntary on both sides; the floor is inviolable regardless of which way the boundary is being drawn.

**Membership obligation — fair share, not forced devotion.** Positive obligation is already in the framework: the close-grade lateral pull (*Individual Worth* §6) — contribute to family, community, society — owed by anyone not actively destructive, scaled by fitness and capacity (more capacity owes more; you do not conscript someone into what destroys them). Its *enforceability* splits cleanly.

- *Legitimate (lightly enforceable):* a fair, reciprocal, capacity-scaled share of a real collective good (defense, infrastructure, care), enforced as *contribution* — a behavior — under *open exit*. The fair-play logic: stay and take the benefits of a cooperative scheme, owe your share of its burdens. Jury duty, taxes, emergency conscription. A scaffolding constraint (Core §9.2), not Rigid, because it compels conduct rather than devotion and because you could leave.
- *Rigid (illegitimate):* the same demand when it reaches for interior loyalty or belief, or falls on a population that *cannot* exit (conscription of captives), or breaches the floor by demanding what destroys the person.

And the correction the intuition needs: morale and binding are an **output** of contribution that is reciprocal and meaningful — not a **lever** you pull by imposing service. Impose obligation *in order to* manufacture cohesion and you have mistaken the readout for the mechanism; that version breeds resentment and hollow compliance, the same failure as mandated diversity or installed self-worth. Service is healthy *when* it is a real share of a real common good that members freely remain in, and the morale follows as a symptom — never the reverse.

**The unification.** Open exit, reciprocal capacity-scaled obligation, conduct-not-conscience enforcement, and a floor that caps both the asking and the expelling — this is the healthy consolidated system. Closed exit, imposed devotion, identity-based removal, and floor-breach — this is the cage. The same boundary, read by whether it holds people through value or through walls.

## XIII. Scope and Next Steps

This paper is the structural repertoire — the diagnosis stage, the seven-plus-one strategies, and the gate. It deliberately stops short of application. The companion **Applications** paper will run specific cases through the diagnosis stage and the Charter — among them market concentration and monopoly, the emergent properties of governing institutions, and regional conflict — because that is where the controversy lives and where it must be handled case by case rather than asserted.

Three threads are flagged open for that work and for revision of this paper:

- **The lethal-substrate case.** Where a coordinating apparatus holds lethal control over its own substrate, Internal Fracture's survivability precondition is not met, and the reintegration pathway is genuinely underdeveloped. This is the hardest case and is deferred deliberately; it should not be the first one attempted.
- **Operationalizing the belief-gap.** The metric in §III is directionally sound but not yet formalized; the observable proxies (defection, samizdat, emigration) need to be turned into something measurable.
- **Manufactured vs. natural windows.** The line between legitimately acting within a natural window and deliberately manufacturing one now has stated conditions (ready attractor, coordination target, genuine emergence mandate) but those conditions need case-by-case stress-testing.
- **When is interference moral? (first treatment now in §XI; full account still pending)** §XI gives the first structural pass — the floor as universal first gate, the separation of warrant-for-response from warrant-for-force, evidence-scaled severity, conduct-not-belief, and the four-quad response. The dedicated *Applications* treatment still owes the harder residue: the threshold of "demonstrated active harm," cross-sovereignty tradeoffs, and the named cases (North Korea's internal suppression; a non-expansionist atrocity) run all the way through.
