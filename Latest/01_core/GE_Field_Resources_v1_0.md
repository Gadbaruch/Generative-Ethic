# The Field

### Researchers and Institutions Working on Adjacent Questions

**Status:** First draft — v1.0
**Series:** Generative Ethic — Reference Pages
**Purpose:** A reader-facing directory of live work the Generative Ethic is in conversation with: people and institutions investigating emergence, complexity, agency, top-down causation, meaning, and the relationship between physics, biology, and value.

---

**A note on what this page is — and is not**

This is a map of the territory, not a roster of affiliations. None of the people or institutions listed below have endorsed the Generative Ethic, and most are unaware of it. They are here because their work bears on the same questions — about how complexity emerges, how higher levels of organization acquire causal power, and how meaning and value relate to the physical world. The relationship currently runs one direction: the Generative Ethic reads them, not the reverse.

The intent is twofold. For a reader, this page shows that these questions are live in serious research, not the private preoccupation of one framework. For the framework itself, it marks intellectual neighbours honestly, so that convergences are visible as convergences and debts are visible as debts.

Entries are grouped by domain. Established academic researchers carry their institution; independently developed frameworks — work produced outside the academy, including the Generative Ethic itself — are marked as such. The label is descriptive, not a ranking: it tells the reader where a body of work sits in the research ecosystem so they can weigh it accordingly.

## Physics, Cosmology, and the Architecture of Emergence

**George F. R. Ellis** — Emeritus Distinguished Professor of Complex Systems, University of Mathematics and Applied Mathematics, University of Cape Town. FRS; Templeton Prize. Co-author with Stephen Hawking of *The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time*. His later work on **top-down causation** — how higher levels of organization exert real causal power over their components — is a direct structural neighbour to the framework's emergence hierarchy. His *On the Moral Nature of the Universe* (with Nancey Murphy) is one of the few serious attempts to ground ethics in cosmology.
→ math_research.uct.ac.za/~ellis/

## Biology, Agency, and Collective Intelligence

**Michael Levin** — Vannevar Bush Distinguished Professor of Biology, Tufts University; Associate Faculty, Wyss Institute, Harvard. AAAS Fellow. Work on **bioelectricity, basal cognition, and collective intelligence**: how cells coordinate into a problem-solving collective that navigates "anatomical space," and how intelligence appears in unconventional substrates. Directly relevant to the framework's substrate-versus-coordination distinction and its treatment of agency across scales.
→ drmichaellevin.org

**Denis Noble** — Emeritus Professor of Cardiovascular Physiology, University of Oxford; CBE, FRS. A founder of systems biology; built the first mathematical model of the heart. His **biological relativity** (no privileged level of causation) and *The Music of Life* argue against gene-centric reductionism in favour of multi-level, feedback-driven organization — adjacent to the framework's hierarchical-network claims and its music work.
→ denisnoble.com

**Terrence Deacon** — Professor of Biological Anthropology and Neuroscience, UC Berkeley. *Incomplete Nature: How Mind Emerged from Matter* develops **teleodynamics** — how purpose and self emerge from constraints on matter through nested dynamical levels (homeo-, morpho-, teleodynamic). One of the most rigorous accounts of how the appearance of ends arises from physics without invoking the supernatural.
→ Faculty page: anthropology.berkeley.edu

## Cognitive Science, Meaning, and Mind

**John Vervaeke** — Professor of Psychology and Cognitive Science, University of Toronto; Director of the Cognitive Science program. Known for **relevance realization**, 4E cognition, and the diagnosis of the "meaning crisis." His work on how agents make sense of an overwhelming world, and on wisdom as the optimization against self-deception, intersects the framework's account of orientation and its psychology paper.
→ johnvervaeke.com

## Cybernetics, Complexity, and Worldview

**Francis Heylighen** — Research Professor, Vrije Universiteit Brussel; directs the Center Leo Apostel (CLEA) and the Evolution, Complexity and Cognition group. Work on **self-organization, the global brain, and the "Third Story"** (with Shima Beigi and Clement Vidal) — an evolutionary worldview proposed to succeed the religious and mechanistic ones. The closest existing project to the Generative Ethic's ambition; the framework positions itself as a complementary extension.
→ pcp.vub.ac.be/HEYL.html

**Boris Shoshitaishvili** — Science studies scholar; 2022–2024 USC–Berggruen Institute Fellow (PhD, Comparative Literature, Stanford; postdoc, UC Berkeley Anthropology). Works on **planetary identity and superorganisms** — reading the Anthropocene, Gaia, and the noosphere as attempts to formulate a planetary self. His engagement with Empedocles and with the planetary superorganism intersects the framework's political-significance work and its discussion of convergence with Empedocles.
→ berggruen.org/people/boris-shoshitaishvili

## Independently Developed Frameworks

These are full interpretive frameworks built outside the academy — structurally parallel projects rather than the empirical research programs above. Listed because the convergences are real and worth a reader's attention, and because intellectual honesty means naming the company the Generative Ethic actually keeps.

**The Paradigm of the Great Life** — Alexander Neshmonin. A dual-force interpretation of reality pairing objective *Energy* with subjective *Information*, scaling agency from molecules to "quasi-organisms," with a text–context–interpretation account of meaning. Independent framework; self-published (*Changing the Paradigm of Life*, ISBN 9798316199631). Notably convergent with the framework's two-force structure and its emergence hierarchy.
→ newparadigmoflife.com

**The Generative Ethic** — Gad Baruch Isaacson. *(This framework.)* Independent framework. Grounds normative ethics in complexity science: goodness as alignment with generative emergence — the trajectory toward richer connectedness that preserves differentiation and future complexity.
→ generative-ethic.gadbaruch.com
